## Referencing: always use UCEM Harvard

All citations and reference lists in this workspace follow *The UCEM Guide to Referencing and Citation* (UCEM Harvard, March 2022, v4.0). Do not use APA, generic Harvard, footnotes, ibid. or op. cit.

### Core conventions
- **Authors:** surname then all initials, no full stops or commas between them: `Bradbury A J`. Use 'and', not '&' (unless '&' is in a title or publisher name). If there's no author, use the corporate body (e.g. RICS, JCT, HM Government); use 'Anon.' only as a last resort.
- **Year:** in round brackets straight after the author, no full stop after it: `Smith J (2017) ...`. Use `(no date)` if there's no date, and `(c.2001)` if the date is approximate. If an author has several items in the same year, add `a`, `b`, ... in the order they're cited.
- **Titles:** standalone works (books, reports, journals, newspapers, contracts, films) in *italics* with Major Words Capitalised. Parts of larger works (chapters, articles, webpages, study resources) go in 'single quotes' in sentence case, not italic.
- **Edition:** `(2nd edn)`, `(4th edn)`. Never state 1st edition.
- **Editors:** `(ed.)` / `(eds)`, lower case, in brackets.
- **Format in square brackets** after the title: `[print book]`, `[e-book]`, `[online]`, `[print]`, `[study resource]`, `[webinar]`, `[video]`, `[forum]`, `[unpublished]`.
- **Publisher:** `Place: Publisher`, e.g. `London: Kogan Page`, `Abingdon: Routledge`.
- **URLs:** `Available at: www... [accessed 28 July 2020]`. No colon after 'accessed'. No full stop after the URL itself. Drop `https://` when the address starts with `www.`. Don't give live links to password-protected PDFs.
- **Page numbers:** in the citation or the reference, never both. Citation form: `(Smith 2004: 54–72)`, using an en dash and no p./pp.
- **Punctuation:** end every reference with a full stop, except where it ends with a URL or an access date.
- **Spelling:** keep a title's original spelling.

### In-text citations
- Narrative: `Phillips and Pugh (2015) recommended ...`. Parenthetical: `(Phillips and Pugh 2015)`. There's no comma between author and year.
- One author: `(Hontoir 2017)`. Two: `(Russell and Leverton 2005)`. Three or more: `(Dixon et al. 2005)`, with *et al.* italic in the text, but every author is listed in the reference.
- Several sources: `(Jones 2016; Smith 2017)`.
- Secondary source: `(Quick 2016: 23; cited in Smith 2017)`. Only Smith goes in the reference list.
- Quotes: in 'single quotes' with page numbers. Quotes longer than two lines become an indented block. Use `...` to cut words and `[ ]` to add them.
- Figures and tables: caption the figure or table, with `Source: Author (Year)` centred underneath. For your own work use `Source: Surname (Year)`, which needs no reference. Use `Adapted from ...` for a modified figure.
- Abbreviated corporate authors: define them once, e.g. `British Standards Institution (BSI) (2003)`, then use `(BSI 2003)`.

### Reference list
- Headed **References**, listing only sources that are cited. It's alphabetical by author, then year (oldest first), then title. All source types go in one list, with no numbering or bullets.
- **Bibliography** (uncited sources) only when asked for.

### Integrity
Never invent or guess a reference. Only cite sources that exist and whose details (author, year, edition, title, publisher) have been checked. If something is unverified, flag it instead of presenting it as fact.
